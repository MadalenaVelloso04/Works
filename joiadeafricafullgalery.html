Jóia de África: Product Design Project

A university project in collaboration with the Aga Khan Foundation focused on strategic rebranding and product development for a Lisbon-based African restaurant.

Rhino 3D Blender Adobe Illustrator
01 / Overview

The Challenge

This project, in partnership with the Aga Khan Foundation, aimed to bolster small business growth through strategic rebranding and improved market visibility.

Our team collaborated with Jóia de África, a lively African restaurant based in Lisbon, to enhance its brand identity and expand its business reach. We developed an in-depth brand book outlining cohesive guidelines—from logos and color schemes to typography and visual style.

The rebranding sought to capture the authentic essence of Jóia de África, a space celebrating African culture and cuisine, and to make it more inviting to a wider audience.

02 / Visual Identity

Concept and Creation of the Brand

Our design process embraced co-creation, testing, and iteration. By grounding decisions in real needs—organization, mobility, security—we created something not just beautiful, but transformative.

Typography System

Our typography reflects the cultural complexity and handmade ethos of Jóia de África. For headlines, we selected bold and expressive fonts that echo the energy of African visual traditions—strong, rhythmic, and geometric. These are paired with clean sans-serif body texts for clarity and balance. A standout element is the use of the custom “A” shared between Jóia and África, both in logotype and signage, which gives the brand a memorable personality. The typography system was designed to remain legible and dynamic across a range of applications—from food packaging to mobile menus—capturing both elegance and practicality.

Color Philosophy

The palette blends cultural symbolism and contemporary aesthetic. We relied on two main tones:
#CA4E29, a vibrant orange, symbolizes warmth, spice, and vitality—evoking both food and fire.
#2C251B, a deep earthy brown, grounds the design in authenticity and natural textures, drawing inspiration from traditional African materials and tones.
These primary colors are supported by complementary reds, greens, and yellows used in physical applications like textiles and signage. Together, the palette evokes heritage, celebration, and craft, avoiding stereotypes while staying emotionally resonant.

03 / Applications

Concept and Creation of the Prototype

We created a functional, portable bag for a working station, featuring three removable drawers, each designed for different storage needs, such as compartments for spices.

To bring this concept to life, we built a high-fidelity prototype at a 1:2 scale, utilizing laser-cut MDF and cardboard for the structure. For a smooth curve at the corners, we applied a custom cutting pattern to bend the wood effectively.

The fabric was decorated with a custom design using the silk-screen technique, and the entire bag received a hand-painted finish with acrylics, adding a unique, artisanal touch.

This station is both practical and portable, allowing chefs to carry essential tools and ingredients with ease, and it captures the distinctive aesthetic of the Jóia de África brand.

Digital Presence

We also envisioned a digital platform to support Georgette’s new role as a private chef. While developing the brand, we considered how a website and social media could extend the project—allowing her to take bookings, share menus, and showcase her work as she travels and cooks in different homes..

04 / Process

Design Journey

As our first product design project, our team felt a strong sense of accomplishment. Every detail—from measurements to painting techniques—was carefully considered and executed.

It was an intense process that challenged us, but we're proud of the results we achieved.

01

Research & Discovery

We started by understanding the story of Jóia de África and its founder, Georgette. This included interviews, cultural research, and analysis of the Lisbon food scene to identify how the brand could evolve beyond its physical space.

02

Concept Development

Exploration of brutalist design principles, testing bold typography combinations, and developing the core brand philosophy.

03

Visual System

Creation of comprehensive brand guidelines, asset libraries, and application standards across all touchpoints.

04

Implementation

Rollout across physical and digital platforms, with ongoing refinement based on real-world application feedback.

The iterative process involved constant testing and refinement. Early concepts were too aggressive and risked alienating potential customers, while later iterations found the right balance between bold and approachable.

Client collaboration was crucial throughout the process. Regular feedback sessions ensured the final identity aligned with business objectives while pushing creative boundaries.
